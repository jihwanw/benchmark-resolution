# Pre-registration — English translation

This is a translation of `PREREGISTRATION_ko.md`, the verbatim Korean text of the three pre-registration stages written in the author's
internal research memo before each experiment. Where the translation condenses a sentence it says so; the Korean text is the record.
The launch records that date the start of each experiment are in `TIMELINE.md`. The pre-registration was an internal document, not an
entry in a public registry, and the memo was not under version control.

## Stage 1 — Killing experiment (written 2026-11-01)

### Design
- Data: MPI Sintel training, 23 scenes, 1,064 frames (existing cache). Preprocessing, resolution (256), training budget (60 epochs),
  and optimiser identical to the earlier JOSA A study.
- Fold design: 5-fold scene cross-validation repeated R = 5 times with mutually independent fold assignments (assignment seeds 201–205).
  In each repeat every one of the 23 scenes is held out exactly once (fold sizes 5, 5, 5, 4, 4); training sets have 18–19 scenes.
  23 scenes × 5 repeats = five different training compositions per scene. (The earlier study's five partitions covered only 16 of 23
  scenes, so they cannot be reused; full coverage is needed.)
- Architectures: H1 primary DirectCNN (14.7 min per run on a T4, measured); H2 auxiliary UNet (24.0 min). Seeds S = 2 (42, 43).
- Runs and cost: 5 × 5 × 2 = 50 runs per architecture; 12.2 + 20.0 = 32.3 GPU-hours; about 5.5 h wall clock on six T4 shards.
- Required implementation changes: (1) a `cvfold` protocol in `make_loaders` with an explicit list of test scenes; (2) per-frame
  evaluation output (`reduction='none'`, test loader `shuffle=False`, scene/frame mapping through `dataset.meta`); (3) besides per-frame
  reflectance MSE and PSNR, the standard Sintel intrinsic metrics si-MSE, LMSE and DSSIM, implemented from the definitions in Grosse et
  al. 2009 and Narihira et al. 2015 (read, not recalled); (4) a version tag in result file names
  (`cv_<arch>_r<rep>_f<fold>_s<seed>_v1.json`); (5) `set -e` in the setup script.
- Primary estimand: the scene score y_u = PSNR of the mean per-frame MSE within the scene (the JOSA A aggregation convention applied per
  scene). Secondary: mean per-frame PSNR, si-MSE, etc.

### Hypotheses and decision rules
- H1 (decomposition): after the crossed random-effects fit, compare the predicted test-side variance V_test = (1 − 5/23)·Ŝ²_γ/5 with the
  partition variance observed in JOSA A (DirectCNN 0.927). Share s = V_test / 0.927 with a bootstrap interval.
  s ≥ 0.7: test side dominates (contribution = design: full-coverage K-fold removal, stratification); s ≤ 0.3: training side /
  interaction dominates (contribution = quantifying "generalisation depends on which scenes were seen"); 0.3 < s < 0.7: mixed — a
  resolution formula reporting both components. In all three cases the project proceeds to the hypothesis stage.
- Death condition: the scene main effect is unstable, σ̂²_scene / (σ̂²_scene + σ̂²_γδ + σ̂²_ε) < 0.5 (scene difficulty reorders with the
  training composition). Then no stable estimand exists for a "resolution from unit difficulty"; record the negative result and
  reconsider the framing. No post hoc model changes to make numbers fit.
- H2 (interaction, UNet − DirectCNN): estimate S²_Δγ and compute the paired MDE. Prediction: for the "3 of 6 pairs reversed" observed in
  JOSA A to hold, S²_Δγ must exceed the seed variance (0.06–0.07); if it is smaller, the reversals come from the training side — report.
- H3 (contrast with the prior prediction): record whether the 95% interval of Ŝ²_γ contains the value implied by a pure test-side model
  (DirectCNN 5.92, UNet 3.35). Whether it does is itself a result.

### Honesty rules
- Null and death results stay in the result files and the session log and are reported in the paper.
- Every aggregate number is generated by script from `results/benchmark_resolution/*.json`; no hand entry. A reproduction PASS is not a
  validation of a definition.
- The existing 325 runs and the new runs must share hardware (T4) and budget to be comparable; if they differ, reproduce at least one
  of the existing partitions in the new runs and calibrate.

## Stage 2 — Main experiment (pre-registration update, written 2026-11-02)

### Design
- Four architectures (direct, unet, transformer, proposed) × R = 5 × 5 folds × S = 3 = 300 runs (≈124 GPU-h; 12 T4 ≈ 10.4 h).
  Primary estimand: si-MSE, LMSE, DSSIM (scene-equal weighting); secondary: PSNR. [This is the amendment: the primary estimand was
  redefined after the killing experiment and before the main experiment.]
- Additional hypotheses: H4 (gauge) — if the interaction under PSNR after aligning the predicted albedo with a per-scene optimal gain
  (si-PSNR) is reduced by ≥ 50% relative to PSNR, the "gain effect" reading is confirmed. H5 — whether TransUNet's seed variance (the
  JOSA A exception, ρ = 1.3) is also large at the scene level. H6 — how much stratified partitions (three strata of per-scene si-MSE
  difficulty) reduce V_test relative to random ones (simulation + realised runs).
- Execution: after the user's approval, on a day that does not conflict with the CVPR submission and the JOSA A decision.

### Launch record (2026-11-02 09:10 KST, approved)
- Grid of 340 runs (300 random + 40 stratified), expected 137.4 GPU-h, 16 T4 shards with LPT balancing (longest 8.75 h). Code
  `experiment_cv2.py`; strata table `strata_v2.json` (v1 DirectCNN si-MSE difficulty, three strata of 8/8/7; a scale-invariant metric was
  used because its v1 ICC was 0.95 against 0.65 for PSNR); analysis `analyze_cv2.py` (all paths validated on synthetic data).
- Additional control: direct/unet seeds 42 and 43 rerun the v1 configurations exactly → measurement of the GPU non-determinism floor
  (same seed, independent runs). [The paper later renamed this the single-run reproducibility component.]
- H4 implementation: per-frame gain sufficient statistics (Σx·x̂, Σx̂², Σx², n) stored so that the scene-pooled optimal-gain PSNR can be
  computed afterwards → reduction of the interaction relative to PSNR.
- H3 extension: six architecture pairs × five JOSA A partitions = 30-point out-of-sample prediction (v1 had one pair, five points,
  r = 0.958).

## Stage 3 — Second benchmark arm: Sintel optical flow (written 2026-11-03; user decision: IJCV target, flow chosen)

Purpose: to ask, in the most controlled form, whether the partition also dominates for a different task on the same 23 units. Data
units and fold assignments (seeds 201–205) are identical to the intrinsic arm, so partition-level scores can be correlated across tasks.

### Design
- Data: MPI-Sintel training, clean pass, 23 scenes, 1,041 pairs. Ground-truth flow, invalid and occlusion masks from
  `MPI-Sintel-training_extras.zip` (3.1 GB; mirror URL to be confirmed from the official downloads page). The .flo header (PIEH tag,
  W, H, interleaved float32 u, v) to be checked against the bundled `flow_code` on the instance before use. Invalid-mask pixels
  excluded; |gt| < 400 valid (torchvision reference training-script convention).
- Models: torchvision `raft_small` (0.99 M) primary, `raft_large` (5.3 M) secondary, both fine-tuned from the C+T pretrained weights
  (Raft_*_Weights.C_T_V2; FlyingChairs and FlyingThings, no Sintel → no leakage), following the standard C+T → Sintel practice with a
  reduced budget. The seed acts only on data order, crops and flips (the initialisation is the shared checkpoint) — the seed factor
  differs in kind from the intrinsic arm (trained from scratch); to be stated.
- Training budget (fixed): 3,000 iterations, batch 4, random 320 × 448 crops + horizontal flip 0.5 (minimal augmentation; the
  photometric and scale augmentations of the standard recipe are omitted — this affects absolute EPE, not the variance question; to be
  stated), AdamW lr 1e-4, wd 1e-5, OneCycle, grad clip 1.0, sequence loss γ = 0.8, fp32, 12 iterations in training and evaluation.
- Evaluation: all pairs of the test fold at full resolution (436 × 1024, 4-pixel bottom padding then crop). Per pair: EPE (primary),
  1/3/5 px outlier rates, Fl-all (EPE > 3 and > 5% |gt|), occluded/unoccluded EPE, mean GT magnitude (difficulty covariate), valid-pixel
  fraction; per-scene aggregation both pair-equal and pixel-weighted.
- Zero-shot baseline: the C+T weights evaluated once on all 1,041 pairs → a per-scene difficulty table of a model that has never seen
  Sintel (a pure test-side covariate).
- Grid: R = 5 (fold seeds 201–205, as intrinsic) × 5 folds × S = 3 (42, 43, 44) × {small, large} = 150 runs + 2 zero-shot runs. No
  stratified arm (following the H6 conclusion). Runtime measured on one calibration instance, then LPT sharding.

### Hypotheses
- F-H1: in the variance decomposition of EPE (scene-equal), a test-side share ≥ 0.7 means "partition dominance is task-general",
  ≤ 0.3 "intrinsic-specific", in between mixed. Same death condition (scene ICC < 0.5 → scene difficulty is not a stable property).
- F-H2: scene-by-method interaction share of raft_small vs raft_large. Same family, so a level like the intrinsic same-family pairs
  (0.00–0.20) is consistent with the "interaction ∝ architectural dissimilarity" hypothesis; a large value rejects it (report honestly).
- F-H3 (cross-task): fold-level correlation between the intrinsic scores of the same (rep, fold) (si-MSE, PSNR, mean of four models)
  and flow EPE. Positive → a common axis of scene difficulty; near zero → task-specific difficulty. Either way material for the
  generality section.
- F-H4: how well the zero-shot per-scene difficulty table explains the post-fine-tuning per-scene difficulty (Spearman). High →
  "difficulty is a data property"; low → "training reorders difficulty".
- The metric-gauge question does not apply to flow (EPE is an absolute scale); instead the 1/3/5 px outlier rates and Fl-all check the
  robustness to the metric convention.

### Honesty and reproducibility rules
- Numbers come from `results/benchmark_resolution/results_flow_v1/*.json` → `analyze_flow.py`; no hand entry. Failed or interrupted
  runs are reported with their logs.
- This arm is for the generality section of the IJCV manuscript. If the result differs from the intrinsic arm (for example, seed
  dominance), it is written as it is — "partition dominance depends on the task and the training regime" is also a finding.
